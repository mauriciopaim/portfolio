Story

From ink on paper
to services at scale

I learned design where mistakes are printed ten thousand times: a print shop in Brazil, setting business cards, books and magazines, and learning color theory the hard way, watching plates come into register.

University in England pulled me into the web. Back in Brazil I was one of three at a young web performance startup, designing the product and writing the front end that made it real. Then three years on a travel SaaS: research, information architecture, testing, the whole loop.

Since 2018 I’ve been in Brussels, designing for telecom, logistics, finance and events, and today the national postal service, where the things I draw are used by millions of people. My work runs the full arc: interviews and service blueprints at one end, design systems and front‑end‑ready specs at the other.

Lately the two ends are joining up. I build what I design, prototyping in real code rather than pictures of it. Six versions of an idea now take the time one used to. Drawing got cheap. Knowing which of the six is right did not, and that is the job. I run the software I design against too, on my own hardware, which is where the fixes I send upstream come from. It feels a lot like the print shop again. Define the system well, and every copy comes out right.

How I work

Three standing rules

  1. 01

    Understand before designing

    Interviews, workshops, service blueprints. I’d rather ask one more question than redesign the wrong thing beautifully.

  2. 02

    Systems over screens

    A good rule outlives a thousand mockups. I write design decisions down so they scale without me in the room.

  3. 03

    Prototype in the real material

    Paper for print, code for software. The closer the prototype is to the material, the more honest the answer.
